# Awesome-Food-Traceability

# Top Food Traceability Platforms Ecosystem
**Curated List of SaaS Products & Open-Source GitHub Projects**
*Focused on Farm-to-Fork Traceability, FSMA 204, Lot Genealogy, Recall Readiness & Supply-Chain Transparency*
**Last updated: September 2026**

This repository tracks notable **SaaS platforms** and **open-source projects** for **Food Traceability**. These systems capture Critical Tracking Events (CTEs) and Key Data Elements (KDEs), enable lot-level genealogy, support rapid recalls, and provide transparency across growers, processors, distributors, and retailers.

**Examples** include Safefood 360°, TraceGains, FoodLogiQ (Trustwell), ReposiTrak, IBM Food Trust, Trustwell, Parsyl, Wherefour, SafeTraces, SG Systems, Wholechain, SMRTR, and iFoodDS (the category leaders).

**Open-source emphasis**: Production food-traceability platforms that meet retailer and regulatory requirements are mostly commercial. Practical open options center on **Hyperledger Fabric** and other blockchain PoCs for supply-chain tracking. This section lists the strongest available open resources and is realistic about the commercial gap.

Contributions welcome! Open a PR to add/update entries. Keep descriptions factual and link to official sites.

## SaaS/Hosted Platforms
- **[Safefood 360°](https://safefood360.com/)**  
  Food safety and quality management platform with strong traceability, lot genealogy, and recall capabilities for manufacturers.

- **[TraceGains](https://www.tracegains.com/)**  
  Supplier compliance and traceability network focused on ingredient and food-brand data exchange and risk management.

- **[FoodLogiQ / Trustwell](https://www.foodlogiq.com/)**  
  Traceability and food-safety platform supporting CTE/KDE capture, FSMA 204 readiness, and farm-to-fork visibility for manufacturers and retailers.

- **[ReposiTrak](https://repositrak.com/)**  
  Supply-chain and food-safety platform used by retailers and suppliers for compliance, tracking, and recall support.

- **[IBM Food Trust](https://www.ibm.com/products/food-trust)**  
  Blockchain-based food-traceability network enabling permissioned sharing of supply-chain data among participants.

- **[Parsyl](https://www.parsyl.com/)**  
  Data and insurance-oriented platform for cold-chain and shipment condition monitoring linked to food risk.

- **[Wherefour](https://wherefour.com/)**  
  Manufacturing and traceability software for lot tracking, genealogy, and compliance in food and related industries.

- **[SafeTraces](https://www.safetraces.com/)**  
  Traceability technology using DNA-based and related markers for precise product and ingredient tracking.

- **[SG Systems / iFoodDS and related plant-floor solutions](https://www.example.com/)**  
  Plant-floor and quality systems that capture production and lot data for end-to-end traceability and recall readiness.

- **[Wholechain, SMRTR and additional specialized traceability networks](https://www.example.com/)**  
  Platforms focused on specific commodities, sustainability claims, or multi-tier supply-chain transparency.

- **[Hyperledger Fabric food-traceability networks](https://github.com/)**  
  Open-source blockchain projects (e.g., FoodTraze, hyperledger-supply-chain examples) demonstrating farm-to-fork tracking on permissioned ledgers.

- **[Tracer and agri-food blockchain governance projects](https://github.com/)**  
  Open blockchain + DAO concepts for supply-chain traceability and decentralized oversight in the agri-food sector.

- **[CTE/KDE data-model open schemas](https://github.com/)**  
  Community and standards-aligned schemas for Critical Tracking Events and Key Data Elements used in FSMA 204-style systems.

- **[QR and consumer-facing open trace viewers](https://github.com/)**  
  Open front-ends that display product journey timelines from scanned codes or public ledgers.

- **[IoT and sensor open collectors for cold chain](https://github.com/)**  
  Tools for capturing temperature, humidity, and location data that can feed traceability records.

- **[Lot and batch open tracking libraries](https://github.com/)**  
  Lightweight open components for recording transformations, splits, and merges of lots in manufacturing.

- **[Recall simulation and audit open helpers](https://github.com/)**  
  Scripts and dashboards for testing recall readiness and generating mock trace reports.

- **[ERP and MES open connectors](https://github.com/)**  
  Adapters that push production and inventory events from open ERPs into traceability stores.

- **[GS1 and EPCIS open tooling](https://github.com/)**  
  Libraries implementing GS1 standards and EPCIS event capture for interoperable supply-chain events.

- **[Documentation and regulatory open playbooks](https://github.com/)**  
  Guides for implementing basic traceability aligned with FSMA, EU, and retailer requirements.

### Additional Strong Open-Source Options
- Prototyping multi-party tracking with **Hyperledger Fabric** or similar permissioned blockchain frameworks.
- Capturing internal lot genealogy with open schemas and open ERP/MES data.
- Accepting that retailer mandates, FSMA 204-scale networks, certified audit trails, and production plant-floor integrations still favor commercial platforms (FoodLogiQ/Trustwell, Safefood 360°, TraceGains, ReposiTrak, IBM Food Trust, iFoodDS, etc.).
- Focusing open-source efforts on interoperability standards, data ownership, and education.

**Frameworks for building custom systems**: Capture CTEs/KDEs at each step → store in a permissioned ledger or relational store → expose genealogy and recall queries → optionally publish consumer-facing QR timelines. Suitable for pilots, research, and single-organization internal tracing. Most food companies that sell into major retail or export markets rely on commercial traceability platforms for compliance and network participation.
